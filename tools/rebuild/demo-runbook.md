"""SHRUTI demo runbook.

The 4-minute on-stage performance. The rebuild recipe gets the system
working; this runbook turns the system into the story.

## Setup (before you walk on stage)

Three tripods, spaced ~1 m apart, at the front of the stage. The
master phone is in the middle; the two elements flank it. The laptop
is on the demo table, lid open, screen mirrored to the master phone
so the jury can read the sync-stats and transcript from a distance.

A teammate sits at the very back of the hall with a third phone (the
"far speaker" for the toggle moment). Their seat is pre-chosen so
their voice lands on the radar from a recognisable direction.

Water. The good luck.

## The 4 minutes

### [0:00-0:20] Silence as the opener

No hello. No introduction. Walk to the centre of the stage, point
at the three phones, and say exactly:

> "These are three phones. Watch them become one."

Then stop talking. Let the silence build. 20 seconds of silence on
stage is longer than you think; use it.

### [0:20-0:50] The toggle

Signal the teammate at the back. They speak normally: "Testing,
testing, one two three." Drowned in hall noise. You flip the
laptop's RAW switch to BEAMFORMED. The teammate's voice surfaces
alone. The radar dot locks. The transcript pane types out in
Hindi (or whatever vernacular the demo profile is).

> "That sentence just travelled 40 feet through 200 conversations."

The "40 feet" is literal: the teammate's chair is ~12 m from the
master phone. The "200 conversations" is approximate; the point is
the room was loud.

### [0:50-1:40] Proof, not claims

Open the sync-stats overlay. Read the live number.

> "Forty-two microseconds. Live number, measured every two seconds,
> all night. It hasn't drifted."

Show the overnight uptime stamp (5 hours of unattended capture, if
the recipe was followed). Then explain the physics in two sentences,
not three:

> "Distance equals delay. Delay equals direction. Direction equals
> silence for everyone except the person you're listening to."

### [1:40-2:30] Hand the power to the jury

Pick the most engaged judge. Invite them to the jury microphone (or
their own seat, depending on venue) and ask them to whisper. The
radar chases them. The transcript types out their words. There is
an applause beat here; let it land, do not talk over it.

### [2:30-3:20] The why

Cover the four use cases, one sentence each:

- Back-bench student hears the professor.
- Journalist in a scrum hears the source.
- Courtroom witness heard clearly.
- Hearing-impaired viewer in a crowd.

Then the thesis:

> "All with phones people already own. Zero new hardware. Zero
> internet. Fully private — nothing ever leaves these devices."

### [3:20-4:00] The close

Land the close word-for-word:

> "Every scoring dimension today was measured on hardware you
> handed us. Your silicon, synchronised into one superhuman ear.
> And the loudest room in this city made it stronger. iQOO's next
> ad shouldn't star one phone. It should star a study group."

Name-drop Most iQOO Usage. Stop talking. Walk to the side of the
stage. The next 30 seconds are the jury's, not yours.

## If the toggle fails

You have 5 seconds to recover. Say:

> "Watch it repair itself."

The sync heartbeat's auto-resync is a designed-in feature. If you
have to narrate it for more than 10 seconds, switch to the
backup stems on the phone. The Tier-0 pitch (2-phone mode) is in
the pre-event checklist. You do not have to apologise.
